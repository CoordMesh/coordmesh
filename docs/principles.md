# Principles

1. **Mechanisms, not institutions.** Model reusable coordination behavior, not particular forms of organization.
2. **Integrate before inventing.** Reuse adequate open standards directly; profile them where shared semantics are needed; invent only for a documented gap.
3. **Research before protocol.** Candidate primitives and transports remain hypotheses until standards and implementations are evaluated.
4. **Transport independence.** Keep domain semantics separate from any first transport or federation technology.
5. **Human authority.** AI may analyze and propose; consequential decisions remain with affected participants unless they explicitly delegate narrow authority.
6. **Contextual trust.** Do not assign universal reputation or worth scores to people. Use contextual claims and attestations.
7. **Identity pluralism.** Distinguish key/device recognition from claims about people or organizations. No state-issued or central identity authority is required by default.
8. **Resilience through useful decentralization.** Serve normal coordination as well as partial disruption and recovery.
9. **Replaceability.** Avoid unnecessary dependence on any one person, organization, forge, service, or infrastructure component.
10. **Spec-first interoperability.** Where appropriate, specify behavior and conformance before implementations.
